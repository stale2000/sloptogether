# Official rules notes — Codex x TwitchCon Hackathon

Source: [Devpost Official Rules](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/rules), accessed 2026-10-23. This is a summary; the linked rules are authoritative and may change.

## Dates

- Submission period: **Oct. 23, 2026, 10:00 a.m. PT through Nov. 2, 2026, 2:00 p.m. PT**.
- Official Rules judging period: **Nov. 5, 2026, 9:00 a.m. PT through Nov. 15, 2026, 5:00 p.m. PT**.
- Winners announced on or around Nov. 15, 2026 at TwitchCon San Diego.
- **Date conflict:** the separate [Devpost schedule page](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/details/dates), saved as `devpost-schedule.html`, lists judging as Nov. 3–6. Follow up with the organizer; the Official Rules page is the primary rules text.

## Who can enter

- Individuals at least 18 or the age of majority where they reside, whichever is older, and teams made up of eligible individuals.
- Organizations and legal entities cannot enter.
- Residents of prohibited jurisdictions are excluded; the rules name Brazil, Quebec, Russia, Crimea, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, comprehensively OFAC-sanctioned countries, and applicable denied-party lists. Eligibility is also barred for event promotion entities, their employees/agents and immediate family/household members, event personnel, judges and their employers, affiliates, and people/entities with conflicts as determined by the sponsor/administrator.
- An individual may join multiple teams and may also enter individually. Each team must appoint an eligible representative who submits on its behalf.

## Project and submission

- Build a **new** project during the Submission Period, for Twitch streamers and their communities, using OpenAI Codex. It must be an interactive experience connected to Twitch (examples: chat, overlay, Twitch API, EventSub, Extension) and work as described. Standard tools, libraries, frameworks, starter templates, and AI coding assistants are allowed; third-party integrations must be properly licensed/authorized.
- Submit a repository URL hosted on GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket, including code, assets, and run instructions. A private repository must be on GitHub or GitLab and shared with the address in the rules.
- Include project description explaining features and Codex use, Codex session ID for the thread used to build most of it, and a YouTube demo video under three minutes showing the project working on Twitch and how it was built with Codex. Video must be publicly viewable (unlisted is acceptable per FAQ), and must not use third-party marks or copyrighted materials without permission.
- Submission materials must be in English or include English translations. Entrants must provide free, unrestricted project access for testing through the end of judging; judges are not required to test and may judge from submitted materials alone.
- Multiple entries are allowed only if each is unique and substantially different. The entrant/team must own the original work and respect third-party IP.
- Submit the completed Devpost entry during the period; saving a draft is not entry. After deadline, submissions generally cannot be changed (narrow organizer-approved exceptions for problematic content).

## Judging and prizes

- Judging can involve expert panels, peer review, automated AI analysis, or a combination. The four published criteria are Innovation/Idea, Potential Impact, Technological Implementation (including Codex use), and Design. The FAQ states two stages: baseline viability pass/fail, then scoring on those four equally weighted criteria.
- $25,000 total: 1st $10,000; 2nd $6,000; 3rd $4,000; two runner-ups at $2,500 each. Each prize includes TwitchCon stage time. Travel/lodging costs are winners’ responsibility; not attending does not forfeit cash. Team prize is paid to representative for allocation. Forms are due within 10 business days after they are sent; prize payment within 60 days after completed forms are received. Winners handle taxes/fees.
- Project IP remains with its developers. Submission gives sponsor a non-exclusive license for judging; sponsor/Devpost may promote submissions and use contributors’ name/likeness/voice/image during the hackathon and for three years thereafter.
